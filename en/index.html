Priests in grassroots communities, pastors on the campaign stage, bishops founding a party. Religion has always played politics in Brazil, on both sides. This route shows how it happened and where the law draws the line.

The turn in numbers

In 1970, nine out of ten Brazilians said they were Catholic. In 2022, just over half. Evangelicals went from 5% to more than a quarter of the population.

The two sides of faith

Religion does not belong to one camp. In recent decades, each side has had its own organized religious base.

Left · Catholics

From grassroots communities to the PT

  • In 1968, in Medellín, the Latin American bishops adopted the "preferential option for the poor".
  • In 1978, the São Paulo Archdiocese's newspaper called for a new grassroots party, and Leonardo Boff said the Base Ecclesial Communities should help form it.The Church hierarchy did not found the PT, but lay people, the Workers' Pastoral and the CEBs took part in organizing it.
  • In 1982, the dioceses of São Paulo and Santo André barred priests and religious from supporting parties.
  • In 1994, Catholics linked to the CEBs were the religious group that voted most for Lula.
  • In 2022, Lula got about 58% of the Catholic vote in the runoff.

Right · evangelicals

From the temple to the campaign stage

  • The Universal Church elects allies through Republicanos, formerly the PRB, known as the "Universal's party". In 2020 that meant 208 mayors and 2,594 city councilors.Church workers hand out campaign flyers at temple doors, and pastors ask for votes at the end of services, according to Agência Pública.
  • In August 2022, Silas Malafaia and other pastors recorded a video calling on the faithful to join Bolsonaro's September 7 rally.
  • In 2022, Bolsonaro got about 69% of the evangelical vote in the runoff.
  • In 2026, Datafolha shows Flávio with 62% of evangelicals in a simulated runoff, against 29% for Lula.

And those who cross the line

  • In October 2022, Lula published a letter to evangelicals defending religious freedom, saying he was against abortion and criticizing the electoral use of faith.
  • In 2026, the PT created the "Evangelicals with Lula" committee and plans more than a thousand committees in poor outskirts.
  • In 2023, the PT let its deputies vote freely on the constitutional amendment expanding churches' tax exemption, and six PT members signed the text. In the final 2026 vote, the PT federation voted against.

The Vatican also put on the brakes

Then-cardinal Joseph Ratzinger published an official critique of the Marxist elements of Liberation Theology. In 1985, the Vatican imposed a year of silence on Leonardo Boff. The resistance did not come only from Washington.

So is the evangelical church a CIA plan?

The sources consulted do not show a plan to create or expand churches in Brazil. Historical accounts of US policy and the use of clergy must be read with their authorship and documentation; they do not prove that general thesis. Researchers explain evangelical growth mainly by local factors, such as urbanization and migration to the outskirts. In Brazil, the largest neo-Pentecostal churches were founded and are run by Brazilians.

What the law allows

Religious leaders can have political opinions. The limit is the use of the temple, the church's money and authority over the faithful.

Electoral campaigning inside churches and templesForbidden

Law 9.504, in article 37, treats temples as places of common use, where campaigning is not allowed. The fine ranges from R$2,000 to R$8,000. Complaints can be filed through the Pardal app.

A church donating money or goods to a campaignForbidden

Article 24 of the same law forbids candidates and parties from receiving donations from religious entities, in money or in goods.

Punishing "abuse of religious power"Indirectly

In August 2020, the TSE rejected 6 to 1 Justice Fachin's proposal to create this punishment, because the law does not provide for it. Religious pressure can still be punished as abuse of economic power, fraud or coercion.

Churches not paying taxesAllowed

The Constitution forbids taxing temples of any faith. In May 2026, the Chamber approved an amendment extending the exemption to purchases by churches and affiliated entities. The text is in the Senate.

Is it true?

Where the proof ends

What is documented

  • The 2022 Census records 56.7% Catholics and 26.9% evangelicals among people aged 10 or older. The older total-population points have a different universe.
  • The Catholic left helped organize the PT, and the Universal Church elects allies through Republicanos.
  • The secondary sources cited report US opposition to Liberation Theology and the CIA's use of clergy; we do not certify here a plan to create Brazilian churches.
  • Campaigning in temples and church donations to campaigns are forbidden.

What is not proven

  • That evangelical growth was planned by the US government.
  • That evangelicals vote as a bloc. Almost a third voted for Lula in 2022.
  • That a specific punishment for "abuse of religious power" exists today. The TSE refused to create one.
